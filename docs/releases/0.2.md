# Genesis 0.2

Genesis is a desktop that can build things: a complete Fedora bootc / KDE Plasma system with local
language models as one of its capabilities. 0.2 is the first version we would hand to a friend.

## What you get

- **A finished desktop.** One identity from boot to login to desktop: colour scheme, wallpaper, fonts,
  dock, splash, lock screen. Flatpaks, Firefox, Konsole, Dolphin, KDE Connect, a toolbox per language.
- **The maker.** Press Meta+Space, say what you want, and Genesis builds it on this machine: a checklist
  app, a timer, a photo-renaming script, a small website. In the default mode it edits the project on its own and asks with a plain-verb card
  before anything outside it; every job ends with Keep / Undo.
- **Models that fit your machine.** First run measures the hardware and offers the most capable pack
  that fits, from a 3.7 GB "tiny" pack for 8 GB laptops to 48 GB GPU packs. Pack definitions are signed
  and verified; every download is checked against its signed checksum.
- **Voice, screen, files.** Hold to talk and hear the reply. Meta+Shift+A asks about a region of the
  screen with a local vision model. Opt folders in and the maker can search your notes and documents.
- **Your phone.** Pair once with KDE Connect; share text starting with "ask" or send a voice note and the
  answer comes back as a notification; run commands from the phone; reply to phone notifications from
  the palette.
- **Nothing leaves the machine** unless you choose Claude Code with your own account, which is one
  click away and clearly labelled.
- **Updates like an appliance.** Signed images, staged in the background, applied on your say-so, with
  rollback. Three flavours: x86_64, x86_64 with NVIDIA, arm64.

## Since 0.2.107 (rolling on the stable channel)

- **A gate on the channel, and everyone's language.** The weekly canary now decides whether the stable
  tags move; a red week holds them. German and Slovenian are complete in the maker, the palette and
  Settings, and a screen reader (Orca) reads every control by name.
- **The Genesis app for Android.** Settings > Phone app shows a code; scan or paste it in the app.
  Your jobs, their permission cards, a make from the phone, one screenshot of the computer on demand.
  Same network, a certificate pinned to this machine, no relay. Verified on a real phone. Builds from
  `android/`; no store listing yet.
- **Babel, the Genesis IDE.** Code-OSS (VSCodium) branded as Babel with the Genesis extension built in:
  the maker in the sidebar on your open folder, permission cards next to the code, ask about the
  selection, change this file, Genesis Dark. Every language VS Code speaks. In the dock.
- **A user guide**, HOW-TO-USE.md.
- **Security follow-ups** from the review: same-origin front door with a per-boot token on the local
  daemons, keyed audit chain, canonical paths before the secrets rules, base images pinned by digest,
  upstream binaries verified by checksum, a RustSec audit in CI.
- **Fixes from the improvement review:** model setup can be reopened ("Set up models" in the menu),
  signed pack definitions are verified at first run as promised, a "no models yet" banner, an
  elapsed-time line on long steps, undo from Settings, keyboard hold-to-talk, checksum links on the
  download page, and a dozen smaller ones.

## Install

- Download page: https://matijagorsek.github.io/genesis/ (ISO, disk images, checksums)
- Try it in a VM first: the qcow2 boots under QEMU, UTM or virt-manager; login `genesis` / `genesis`.
- Already on Genesis 0.1: nothing to do, the `stable` channel carries you forward.

## Known limits

- Verified in virtual machines (x86_64 under KVM, arm64 under Apple's hypervisor) and against an
  Android emulator; the first real-hardware reports are welcome as "Hardware report" issues.
- Spoken replies are English only. The interface is partly translated to German and Slovenian (first run, palette, maker start page).
- The maker on CPU-only machines is slow with the larger models; the tiny pack answers in seconds and
  extends templates well, but writing a whole app from nothing wants a GPU.
- Phone notification replies are wired but were not observed in the emulator; a real phone will tell.

## Under the hood

Fedora bootc 44, Plasma 6, llama.cpp behind llama-swap, whisper.cpp, Piper, KDE Connect, bubblewrap
sandboxes, a permission broker with an adversarially reviewed policy. Rust daemons, Qt WebEngine
window, a native System Settings module. Everything is in the repository, Apache-2.0.

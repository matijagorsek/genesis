2 · What can be improved (already there, not yet great)

  1. The first ten minutes. Start the desktop before the download finishes, run the first make on the small model the moment its 1.2 GB lands, and make the wizard's last step a live make rather than three offers. Target: first thing made within ten minutes on a 50 Mbit line.
  2. The maker on small models. The evaluation is the yardstick. Next levers: a shorter compact script per template, a "run first, then edit" order, and the model choosing from three canned plans instead of free planning. Target: 7 of 10 on the tiny pack.
  3. Rule 4, finished. The mode on the status widget and in the palette header, Meta+Shift+M cycles it, and per-app always/never in Settings backed by the policy overlay that already exists.
  4. Rule 8, finished. Esc stops speech everywhere, a spoken "install" or "delete" shows a visual confirm before it runs, and a wake word as an opt-in with a visible mic.
  5. Voice replies in German and Slovenian. Piper has voices for both; the packs are data. One evening.
  6. Proactive cards, wider. Today: failed runs, calendar files, staged updates, uncommitted repos, phone pairing. Add: a document changed by someone else, a photo folder with no album, a laptop battery habit, a printer offline.
  7. Settings, shorter. Fourteen sections is a lot. Group into four: Machine, Genesis, Phone, Advanced.
  8. The phone app on the release page and a store. The signing key into repository secrets, an APK on every release, then F-Droid.

3 · Ten moments that would make people say "wait, what?"

Ranked by how hard they land divided by effort. S is a day, M a week, L more. "Ground" says what already exists to build on; none of these starts from nothing.

1. Show me, then do it needs GPU L · ground: ask about the screen, browser control, the permission tiers

Copilot Vision could look; nothing on Linux can look and then act, locally, with a permission card.
Press Meta+Shift+A, drag over any window, say "fill this form with my address" or "put these numbers into a spreadsheet". The vision model reads the region, the agent drives the app through AT-SPI (the accessibility tree Orca already sees), every action a plain verb on a card. The browser tools become the general case.

2. The morning card built M · ground: proactive cards, the system tools, the chat

Proactive beats a chat window; one card that is right every morning is the habit that keeps a desktop.
First login of the day: one card in the maker and on the phone. "Update waiting, applies at restart. Two things you made yesterday were never installed. Battery habit: you charge at 8%. Wi-Fi at the kitchen table is weak." Every line from a local tool, every line with a one-click action.

3. Say it, it happens built as press-speak-done; a wake word waits for a model trained on the word M · ground: hold-to-talk, whisper, Piper, the palette

Push-to-talk is a demo; a wake word that is visibly off by default and visibly on when you choose is a daily thing.
"Genesis, dark mode." "Genesis, what did I write about Lisbon?" Wake word on device (openWakeWord, CPU), a mic badge in the tray whenever it listens, Esc stops any reply, and a spoken request that would install or delete shows a card before it runs. Replies in the desktop's language.

4. Fix my computer built S · ground: the sixteen system tools, the chat

Every other assistant explains; this one has the tools and the permission cards to act.
"My Wi-Fi is slow" gives the diagnosis in one sentence and a button: "Reconnect", "Forget and rejoin", "Use the cable". "The printer does not print": find it, set it up, print a test page. "My battery drains": the top three processes and an offer to switch the power profile. Ship as chat templates with the tools behind them.

5. Make it while I sleep built M · ground: the maker's API, recipes, the evaluation harness

A slow CPU machine can still build a lot overnight; nobody offers a queue.
Queue five makes or a recipe in the evening; the maker runs them one by one on the small model with the plan card pre-answered, snapshots everything, and the morning card says what worked. On a laptop, only on mains.

6. One picture needs GPU L · ground: the pack format, the router, the gallery

The first time a made app appears in the menu with an icon nobody drew, people take a screenshot.
A "picture" pack role (Z-Image-Turbo, Apache 2.0, 8 steps, fits 8 GB) behind a small stable-diffusion.cpp service; the maker generates an icon and a wallpaper for every app it installs, and the chat can make an image on request. GPU packs only, off on CPU.

7. Phone and desktop, one thing built M · ground: the companion app, KDE Connect, sessions

Continuity is what people miss most from the platforms they left.
Start a make on the phone on the bus, see the steps, answer a card; at the desk the same session is open in the maker. "Send this to my phone" in the palette for any text, link or file; "open this on the desktop" from the phone. Already half there through sessions and the share endpoint.

8. Time machine built M · ground: genesis-txd snapshots, the undo history

Undo exists; seeing it is what makes people trust Hands-off mode.
A timeline of every job with before-and-after diffs of the files it touched, a scrubber to any point, and "restore just this file". The audit chain is already there; this is the view.

9. Babel, inline built M · ground: the Babel extension, the maker sessions, completion

Completion is table stakes; "select, describe, see the diff" is what makes an editor feel like a colleague.
Ctrl+I on a selection: a one-line field, the maker rewrites just that range, the diff appears inline, Tab keeps it, Esc drops it. Then "explain the error" on any diagnostic. Runs on the coder model where there is one.

10. The sixty-second demo built S · ground: everything

The distros people install are the ones whose video they saw. There is no video yet.
A "Show me Genesis" entry in the menu that runs a scripted minute: pull the cable (network badge stays green), say "a checklist app", watch the steps, install it, ask about the screen, undo. Also the script for the launch video, recorded from the same entry so it is never staged.

6 · What we still do not do

No always-on capture, no AI button on every surface, no cloud fallback by default, no features announced before they run. The wake word is off until chosen and visibly on when it listens. "Show me, then do it" acts only on the region you drew, only after the card. A timer must still be a timer.

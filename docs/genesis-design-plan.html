Genesis · design plan · updated 14 September 2026

What has to be true for Genesis to be mind-blowing

Three research passes (Plasma visual identity, what wows people in 2025–26 products, and agent interaction design) boiled down into one identity stack, twelve moments, ten rules, and an order of work.

1 · The look: one identity, everywhere

The distros people screenshot at minute one (Omarchy, Aurora) win with coherent defaults, not with replaced widgets. Identity comes from colour, wallpaper, type, panel and boot-to-login continuity. Breeze stays; everything around it becomes Genesis.

Colour scheme: Genesis Dark

Near-black neutrals with a blue bias, one teal accent, violet as the only second colour. Ships as a real KDE colour scheme, so every Qt app, Kirigami app and the login screen follow it.

Wallpaper: First Light

Procedural: luminous ribbons over a deep field, a horizon glow, faint stars. Rendered at 2560×1600 and 4K. The same image is the boot, splash, login, lock and desktop background.

Type: Inter + IBM Plex Mono

Inter for the interface at 10pt, Plex Mono for code and the terminal. Both packaged in Fedora, both open licences, set system-wide with fontconfig aliases.

Icons and sound

Papirus-Dark icons (packaged, complete coverage), Ocean sound theme, Breeze cursors. No third-party window decoration or Kvantum: Plasma 6.5 already rounds every window at the compositor.

Panel: a floating dock

Centered, fit-to-content, floating at the bottom. Genesis mark as the launcher, the maker next to it, five pinned apps, tray, clock. KRunner free-floating in the middle of the screen.

Boot to login, one composition

Plymouth spinner carries the Genesis watermark, the Plasma splash shows the mark on the wallpaper with a thin progress line, the login manager uses the same wallpaper and colours.

built colour scheme, wallpaper, fonts, icons, sound, dock layout, splash, boot watermark, system defaults under /usr/share/kde-settings. Verified in the VM and in the CI captures of every release.

3 · Ten rules for how it behaves

  1. Meta+Space is the one door. Typing, holding to talk, and “do this with the selection” all live in the same palette. Starter prompts sit under the empty field.
  2. A plan card before anything touches the system. built · a plan card with chips and steps before the job starts (Ask and Trusted modes) “Will touch: these files, this app, the network” as chips, then Proceed / Edit / I’ll do it myself.
  3. Plain verbs, never tier names. “Genesis wants to install a package. Allow once · Allow for this project · Not now.” Tiers live in Settings.
  4. Three modes on the status bar, one key to cycle: partial: a selector on the start page Ask · Trusted · Hands-off. Per-app always/never beats the classifier.
  5. Every job runs on a snapshot and ends with Keep / Undo. A timeline lists jobs; irreversible steps (sent, deleted remotely) are marked in red.
  6. Show the thing being made, live. built · steps, and an elapsed-time line after ten seconds Steps with checkmarks, never a spinner alone; a stalled step says why after ten seconds.
  7. “On this machine” is provable. A tray indicator shows the model and “no network used”; any download is listed per job.
  8. Voice: acknowledge in 300 ms, transcript first. partial: no Esc-to-stop, no visual confirm for risky spoken intents Push-to-talk by default, wake word opt-in with a visible mic, risky spoken intents get a visual confirm, Esc stops speech.
  9. First run ends with something made. Three one-line offers; the result lands in a “Made here” gallery with a shareable recipe.
  10. Accessible by construction. Keyboard-complete, screen-reader status region, reduced-motion respected, every sound has a visual twin.

5 · What we deliberately do not do

No screen recording or always-on capture (Recall). No AI button on every surface (Copilot). No cloud fallback by default (Siri). No replaced window decorations or Kvantum (Garuda’s breakage). No features announced before they run (Siri again). Basics first: a timer must still be a timer.
